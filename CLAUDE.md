# CLAUDE.md

This file provides guidance to Claude Code (claude.ai/code) when working with code in this repository.

## Project

Personal website for Cristian Marino, founder of BoostBrands (boostbrands.cl). Two static pages:

- `index.html`: a page that sells BoostBrands' services to companies (not a résumé), ordered as a sale: hero (booking + WhatsApp only), company band, "¿Te suena?" with six client situations in Cristian's words, each with how he tackles it, and one booking button, services and how I work (`#boostbrands`: three cards, each with a short line and `.card-tags` at its foot listing what it covers; steps `#como-trabajo`), skills ("Lo que llevo a tu empresa", `#habilidades`), "Casos de éxito" (projects accordion, `#proyectos`), "Resultados, no promesas" (about + stats, `#sobre-mi`), track record as a scroll timeline (`#path`: kept on purpose, it gives his approach weight; newest to oldest, the line draws as you scroll and the entry at mid-screen is highlighted; edit entries in the markup, both languages. Entries show roles and scope only: the figures live in the success stories, and each stage links to its cases with `.path-case` pills (`data-case` = index in `PROJECTS`, opened with `openCase()`)), education, contact. Menu: Servicios · Habilidades · Casos de éxito · Cómo trabajo · Trayectoria · Contacto; the full CV is linked from the footer and the timeline only. On phones a booking bar (`#sticky-cta`) shows between the hero buttons and the contact section.
- Wording: address the client as "empresa" / "tu empresa" (EN "company", "business"), never "PYME"/"SME" on the home page; Cristian doesn't want to play his profile down.
- `cv.html`: interactive CV aimed at executive/academic audiences (career timeline, achievements, network, gallery, education).

There is no build step, package manager, framework or test suite. Each page is self-contained HTML with its CSS in a `<style>` block and vanilla JS in a `<script>` at the end of `<body>`.

## Shared pieces

- `config.js` sets `window.SITE_CONFIG` (contact email, booking link, WhatsApp number). Both pages load it and fill every `[data-agenda]`, `[data-whatsapp]` and `[data-email]` element from it. Empty `calendarUrl` makes "Agenda" buttons fall back to a `mailto:`; empty `whatsapp` keeps WhatsApp buttons hidden. Booking buttons read "Agenda una reunión de 30 min para conocer tu problema" (EN "Book a 30-min call to understand your challenge"); the phone booking bar uses the short "Agenda una reunión de 30 min". Cristian dropped the word "diagnóstico" from them.
- Accessibility: on both pages everything between the nav and the footer sits in `<main id="main">`, reached by a keyboard-only skip link (`.skip`, "Saltar al contenido"); keep new sections inside it.
- `404.html` is the branded page Vercel serves for missing paths (same tokens, language and theme preferences).
- SEO: both pages have a canonical link; index.html carries JSON-LD (Person + ProfessionalService) built from CV facts only; `robots.txt` and `sitemap.xml` list the two pages.
- `fotos/retrato.jpg` is the portrait used in both heroes. `og.jpg` (1200×630) and `favicon.svg` are referenced from both `<head>`s.
- Language and theme preferences are shared through localStorage keys `lang` and `theme`.

## Languages (ES default, EN toggle)

- Static text is written twice as sibling elements with `data-l="es"` / `data-l="en"`; CSS hides the one not matching `html[lang]`. Add both versions whenever you add copy.
- In `cv.html`, dynamic content comes from JS data arrays (`ROLES`, `WINS`, `CATS`, `GROUPS`, `COUNTRIES`, `EDU`, `TICKER`, `ROLE_LINES`, `PHOTOS`). English values live in sibling fields with an `_en` suffix and are read through `T(obj, key)`; `setLang()` re-renders every section. Edit content in those arrays, not in the markup.
- Default language is the saved preference, else Spanish for Spanish-language browsers and English otherwise.

## index.html skills section

- "Lo que llevo a tu empresa" (`#habilidades`, kicker "Habilidades") is rendered by `renderSkills()` from the `SKILLS` array (same `_en` convention): one tab per category, in the order Cristian gave (Marketing, Ventas, Operaciones, Inteligencia artificial, Organización, Estrategia). Each has `name`/`sub` for its tab, `title`, `skills` (Cristian's own skill names, shown as pills under the title; his list has 20, e.g. "Pricing (EVC, análisis conjoint)", "Dynamic Work Design", "Three Lenses"), a `for` line shown as "Para tu empresa" and three `proofs`. A proof is `{ at, who, text, case? }`: `at` is a key of `SK_ORGS` (the companies strip, Cencosud 2008 → MIT Sloan 2024, lit for the companies the category's proofs name), `who` the label, and `case` the index in `PROJECTS` of its success story ("Ver caso" opens that card and scrolls to it). Proofs come from the CV only: no skill levels or percentages, and prefer facts the page doesn't already show as figures.
- Tabs are an ARIA tablist (arrow keys, Home/End). The categories are a deck of cards (`.sk-card`, all in one grid cell so switching never moves the page): `data-d` from `skDepth()` puts the front card at 0, the next two peeking out below (1, 2), the previous one flown out to the left (`out`) and the rest hidden (`back`). The front card can be dragged or swiped sideways (past 70px it moves to the next or previous card; vertical scrolling still works through `touch-action: pan-y`), and there are ‹ › buttons above it. With a mouse it tilts and a light follows the pointer, and hovering a proof lifts its company in the strip (`.ping`). On phones the tabs become one sideways-scrolling row of chips. While the section is on screen it tours the categories every 8 s (story-style bar on top, `skRun`), paused while the pointer is over the deck and stopped for good when the visitor picks one, drags a card, swipes the chips or presses pause; no tour, tilt or card motion under reduced motion. On wide screens a booking card fills the bottom of the tabs column.
- Below it, "Experiencia en industrias" (`#industrias`, static markup in both languages): six flip tiles (Fintech, Automotriz, Consultoría de gestión, Retail, Tecnología, Innovación). The front shows only its icon and its name at the bottom, with a large faint copy of the icon behind it (`.ind-wm`, added by JS) (no logos: Cristian wants logos only where they can look uniform); hover, keyboard focus or a tap (`aria-pressed`, one at a time) turns it over to a navy back naming where he worked in it. Under reduced motion the back replaces the front without turning.

## index.html projects section

- "Casos de éxito" (kicker "Lo que puedo hacer por tu empresa") is a horizontal accordion of `<details class="proj" name="proyectos">` rendered by `renderProjects()` from the `PROJECTS` array in the page script (same `_en` field convention as cv.html). One card is always open; at 1365px and below it becomes a vertical accordion (wider screens use a fixed height, `--proj-h`, and long cards scroll inside rather than clip). Each project lists `logos` (keys of `LOGOS`): shown beside the visual on wide screens (skipped when the diagram already shows them all), above the text when stacked, and the first one on the closed tab.
- Opening a card keeps it where its tab was clicked (`d._y` in the toggle handler): in the stacked layout the card that closes sits above, and without this the page jumped down toward the education section.
- Every project shows its results as `stats`/`stats_en` ([[value, label]]): big figures that count up from zero when shown (`data-to`; the about section's counters use `data-count`, keep them apart). All figures in a card share one size that fits the longest (`--n`). `bullets` are optional and only for qualitative points; don't repeat a figure that the stats or the visual already show.
- Before/after panels are built with `dFlip(aria, [before tab, after tab], ...parts)` and parts `fStores` (store icons, some kept), `fWaffle` (100 squares, lit = share), `fSwap` (text that changes), `fNew` (pills that appear after) and `fMetric` (bar + value). They play before → after when they come on screen or their card opens, and the tabs switch them. Project 02: 20 franchises → 4 company stores, e-commerce and MotoFin, receivables and credit bars. Project 06: premium share 1% → 5% with Michelin chefs and the LATAM line-up.
- Depth in the projects band: the navy band has an orange/blue glow and a faint dot grid; cards carry real shadows and closed tabs lift on hover and show the project's first stat (`.p-fig`). Every `.dg` visual is a dark "stage" inside the white card: it redefines the colour tokens (`--bg`, `--surface`, `--border`, `--muted`, `--heading`…) on itself so the helpers inside need no dark variants; keep new diagram styles on those tokens.
- Each project has a `pyme`/`pyme_en` line (shown as "Para tu empresa" / "For your business") translating the case into what it means for the client's company. The section sits on a navy band (`#proyectos.on-navy`).
- Projects without photo or video show a schematic (`sketch: t => diagram(aria, ...parts)`) labelled "Esquema con cifras reales": real logos and CV figures only, built from helpers (`dCap`, `dTier`, `dNode`, `dDown`, `dStep`) showing how the project worked (01 brands → BoostBrands → channels, 03 Shopify → HubSpot → online channel); results go in the stats, not in the diagram. Inside diagrams, titles (`dCap`) use the display font like headings and labels (`.dg-lbl`, metric labels) use the body font in sentence case; no small mono capitals there. A diagram is one `role="img"` with a summary in `aria`. Its parts reveal in order the first time it is on screen and replay when its card opens, and a dot keeps running down each arrow while the card is open (all skipped under reduced motion). The wide-screen visual column is only ~300px, like a phone; container queries in `.dg` handle both. Retail channels in project 01 are Falabella, Paris and Mercado Libre (`logos/falabella.png` and `logos/paris.png` from Cristian, `logos/mercadolibre.svg` from the svgl collection; `dNode('', key)` shows a logo alone when it already reads as the name). A project can also carry `gallery` ({ title, title_en, items: [{ src, alt, alt_en, pos? }] }): real photos in a row under its text that open in the `#lb` photo dialog (project 01 shows Acapella at Falabella). If `fotos/proyecto-NN.jpg` exists (NN = 01…07, the project's position), the real photo replaces the schematic automatically. A project can carry `clip` ({ src, poster, label, photo? } for a self-hosted video in `videos/`, optionally with a photo above it, shown instead of the schematic; the drone race uses its 2017 phone video and team photo) and a `link` ({ href, label, label_en }) shown as a source link under the chips (the drone race links its Ingeniería UC article). A project with a `videos` list (YouTube id, optional playlist id, `label`/`label_en`) shows those videos instead: a thumbnail that becomes a youtube-nocookie player on click. Such a project can also carry `photo` ({ src, alt, alt_en, cap, cap_en, credit, pos?, inset?: { src, alt, alt_en, pos? } }), shown above its videos with the optional second photo over its bottom-left corner; both open in the `#lb` photo dialog. Project 04 shows the La Nación (Paraguay) photos (`fotos/paraguay-lanzamiento.jpg` with `fotos/paraguay-entrevista.jpg` as inset, credited "Fotos: La Nación, 2019") and links that article.

## cv.html specifics

- `ROLES` drives both the career Gantt chart (`start`/`end` as decimal years, `lane` picks the row) and the detail panel shown when a bar is clicked.
- `WINS` entries with `top: true` lead the achievements grid as large navy tiles while the "Todos" filter is active. `GROUPS` keeps tools (Shopify, HubSpot, Salesforce) in their own "Herramientas" group, separate from brands and partners. Each `GROUPS` item is `{ n, rel, rel_en, logo?, bg?, mono }`: `logo` points to a file in `logos/` shown on a white tile (or on `bg`, for logos drawn on a brand color such as Mademsa); without one (or if it fails to load) the tile shows the `mono` initials on navy. Accenture, 3M, Shopify, HubSpot and Salesforce come from Simple Icons and gilbarbara/logos (both CC0); the rest are files Cristian supplied, trimmed (wide logos with unreadable text keep only their symbol). Add others as files in `logos/` and set `logo`.
- The hero uses `fotos/mit-sloan.jpg` in a landscape frame so it differs from the home page portrait.
- The gallery only shows photos that actually load; the `#fotos` section and its nav link stay hidden until at least one `PHOTOS` entry exists in `fotos/`.

## Conventions

- Palette is orange + navy, defined as CSS custom properties on `:root` and redefined for dark mode both under `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` (guarded by `:root:not([data-theme="light"])`) and under `:root[data-theme="dark"]`; keep the two dark blocks in sync and keep both pages' tokens aligned.
- Section titles (`h2`) put one word in `<em>`, rendered in Instrument Serif italic in the accent color on both pages. Each figure should appear once per page; avoid repeating the same number in hero, badges and stats.
- Depth over flat fills: key surfaces use soft layered shadows and light (orange/blue radial glows, a faint dot grid) rather than plain boxes. The hero highlights its accent word with a marker stroke and lights the portrait (orange/blue glow faded at the sides, a dot pad behind its corner); its headline rises word by word on load (JS wraps words in `.w`), and one glass credential card sits on the portrait (MIT Sloan MBA; Cristian removed the Country Manager and CMO cards), drifting with the mouse (`--px`/`--py`, `--k`). His roles go in the lead instead (Country Manager Bajaj Chile y Paraguay, CMO regional LATAM, Director de Marketing y Ventas Bajaj Motochile, marketing at Electrolux, Accenture and Cencosud), followed by a `.why` card with three reasons to work with him (done it, multinational thinking at your scale, execution together with AI); on phones that card moves below the buttons. Keep anything around the portrait inside the viewport (transforms count as overflow) rather than clipping the hero, which would cut the portrait's shadow. On phones the portrait comes first, small, with the card beside it, and the lead switches to a short version (`.lead-s`). Under the buttons a trust line reads "Por videollamada · Sin compromiso"; "¿Te suena?" cards carry a large serif quote mark and a tinted "Cómo lo abordo" footer, with a navy CTA bar; the about stats sit on one glowing navy panel (`.stats`) and only show facts the page doesn't show elsewhere (50 empresas asesoradas, ~200 personas, Mejor CX de LATAM, Premio global Electrolux 2015; word stats use `.stat.award`). Keep new sections in that language.
- Background charts (index.html): two decorative growth shapes with no numbers, labels or axes, so they show no data and claim nothing. `#hero-chart` is a hockey-stick growth line (SVG, path built in JS) behind the bottom of the hero, flat under the text and climbing in the gap before the portrait: it draws in on load and, with a mouse, a dot follows the pointer along it while the line to its left lights up (`#hc-rect` clip); on phones it only fills the bottom third. `#bars-chart` is a row of rising bars with a trend line behind "Resultados, no promesas": they grow left to right as the section scrolls in (`--g`) and the bar under the pointer lights up (`.hot`/`.warm`). Both fade out at their edges with masks and are static under reduced motion.
- Background art (index.html): sections with class `art` get topographic lines (`fondos/contornos.svg`, generated, used as a mask so their colour is the `--art-ink` token) faded by `--art-fade`, and with `data-photo` one of Cristian's photos as a watermark (`fondos/bg-*.jpg`: navy/white duotones, blurred and compressed; loaded by JS only when the section gets close). Per-section knobs (`--art-pos`, `--art-size`, `--art-repeat`, `--photo-pos`, `--photo-size`, `--photo-mask`) sit next to `.more` in the CSS; `--photo-op`/`--photo-blend` are theme tokens (multiply on light, screen on dark). Photos used: Acapella at Falabella (Qué hago hoy), Stanford GSB (formación), the Stanford classroom nameplate (contact box). Cards over a photo use `glass` / `glass-kids` (translucent surface + blur). Make new duotones from photos he supplied, never stock images.
- Contrast rules: `--accent` (#E8551C) is for fills and large numbers only; small accent text uses `--accent-text` (#C2410C, 4.75:1); text on orange buttons uses `--accent-ink` (dark), never white.
- Layout must work at phone width (≈390px) with no horizontal overflow; check desktop and mobile after visual changes. Tap targets are at least 44px.
- Site copy comes from Cristian's CV. Keep figures consistent with it and never invent achievements, numbers, clients or testimonials.
